# Concept: Data commoditisation

> B2B contact data has become a near-free commodity. The competitive advantage shifts from owning data to distribution and execution: reducing the distance between signal and action.

## The argument

[[expert-adam-robinson]] argues contact data is commoditised and prices [[tool-moltsets]] accordingly ($27/mo unlimited vs a $99-per-5,000-credits standard). He can do this because the data asset is cross-subsidised by three other business lines, giving near-zero marginal cost per contact. He credits 5x5 with already commoditising B2B data.

The corollary: if data is cheap, the moat moves elsewhere.
- **Distribution as moat** -- founder brand, UGC, and an internal validation engine, not the rows. A stolen database changes nothing.
- **Execution as moat** -- as a commenter (Adam Behr) put it, "data is becoming infrastructure, execution is becoming the moat"; advantage comes from reducing the distance between signal and execution.

## Implications for GTM engineering

- Compete on workflow speed and signal-to-action latency, not data volume.
- Cheap data fuels agentic sourcing and enrichment at scale (see [[concept-agentic-sourcing]], [[concept-waterfall-enrichment]]).
- Expect an explosion of "vibe-coded" GTM apps as data approaches free.
