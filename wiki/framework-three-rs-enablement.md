# Framework: Retrieve, Recall, Resist

> [[expert-mercy-bell]]'s definition of what sales enablement actually is, surfaced in [[expert-brendan-short]]'s The Signal. Most teams over-invest in the easy first R and neglect the two that separate top performers.

## The three Rs

- **Retrieve.** Can your people find what they need (information, content, competitive intel)? The systems-and-tools question, the easiest, the one most companies default to, largely RevOps-ownable.
- **Recall.** Can a rep, in the moment, pull up the right heuristic, match tone to context, read the room? Pattern recognition built through repetition, not a knowledge-base article. This is what separates top performers.
- **Resist.** Judgment and impulse control: knowing when not to oversell, when to walk from a non-ICP deal, when to shut up and listen. "Discernment and judgment is mostly about what we don't do, not what we do."

Most enablement teams have it inverted: nearly all their time on retrieve, almost none on recall or resist.

## Why it matters now

The 2010s enablement model (content person + systems person + trainer, keep the LMS fresh) is dead. Recall and resist are exactly the human skills that AI agents do not supply and that become the execution moat as teams shrink. The companion idea -- the **agent parallel** -- is that we lavish care on agent context windows and graded outputs while neglecting the humans; the same rigour should go into ramp, coaching, and leadership. See the four-program architecture and self-determination-theory grounding in [[source-brendan-short-renaissance-of-enablement]].
