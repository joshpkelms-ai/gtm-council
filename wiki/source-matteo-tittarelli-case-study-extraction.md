# Source: Matteo Tittarelli -- Granola transcript to sourced case-study draft

> LinkedIn post by [[expert-matteo-tittarelli]]. Raw: `raw/Matteo Titt/5.md`. Anchor source for [[play-case-study-extraction]].

## Summary

A case-study draft left in your to-dos is a compounding cost: every week you wait, the source material loses value. Week 1, the verbatim quote is fresh in the recording and the metrics are in last week's QBR. Week 4, the recording is buried in [[tool-granola]] and the metrics are in a stale sheet. Week 8, the writer falls back on "Company X was struggling with productivity. Now they're 50% faster" -- nobody can source the 50%, and the study reads like every other one on the internet.

His fix: a structured extraction the moment the engagement closes. The skill he runs takes a Granola transcript and pulls a quote library (3-5 verbatim quotes with attribution), a metrics table where every number traces to a source, and a flagged "signature moment" (the specific thing the customer said that makes the story feel real). Anything missing gets `[METRIC NEEDED]` or `[PLACEHOLDER]` -- the story never ships with invented numbers. Time from transcript to draft: about one minute, versus 4-6 hours and weeks of meandering edits before.

## Key takeaways

- Customer-story source material decays on a weekly clock; extract within ~48 hours or you are writing fiction (commenter Richard Wilson).
- Trace every number to a source; mark gaps with placeholders rather than inventing. This is [[concept-claim-tagging]] applied to content.
- The output is a sourced draft scaffold (quotes, metrics-with-sources, signature moment), not a finished puff piece.
