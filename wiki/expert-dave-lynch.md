# Dave Lynch

> Leads a GTM engineering team at Fin (fin.ai / Intercom), writing on the Fin "ideas" blog. First-hand account of standing up a GTM engineering function: pair a builder with a domain expert, build the orchestration layer above your systems of record, and build for the flex, not the forecast.

## Who he is

Runs a new engineering team at Fin dedicated to GTM effectiveness and efficiency. Publishes on ideas.fin.ai.

## Core positions

- **The GTM engineer role is real, not a hypothesis.** "Embed a builder next to a domain expert, give them a fast feedback loop, and get out of the way." Cites Will Jones at Attio describing the job as "directing Agents to do it well" rather than doing the work directly.
- **Buy systems of record, build the orchestration layer.** Keep Salesforce, Stripe, Fin; the advantage is in the connective tissue between them, now buildable in weeks not six-month implementations. See [[concept-orchestration-layer]].
- **Jevons paradox for GTM.** Quotes Nicolas Sharp (Attio): as AI gets more capable, demand for people who build and operate it grows, not shrinks.
- **Build for the flex, not the forecast.** Don't predict 18 months out; build an org so agile it doesn't matter if the vision is half wrong. Honest about the tension with near-term pipeline pressure and whether today's "cockpits" are durable or sophisticated throwaways.

## Field results (as reported)

An AEO engine that grew bot-sourced pipeline; an outbound "cockpit" over Outreach + data warehouse + internal infra generating $1.2M pipeline, tracking toward $5M by year-end; an internal tool built in ~3 weeks that replaced a vendor product under evaluation.
