# Source: Eric Nowoslawski -- The list is the message

> LinkedIn post by [[expert-eric-nowoslawski]]. Raw: `raw/Eric Nowoslawski/7.md`. The anchor source for [[concept-list-is-the-message]].

## Summary

When a list is segmented precisely enough, the email almost writes itself; when it is not, you write clever copy to paper over not knowing who you are talking to. Most platforms only segment by what a company is (industry, size, tech stack), which rarely changes the message on its own. CEO vs founder does not change what you say. Industry often does not either.

What changes the message is who they sell to. A company selling into legal teams lives in a different world from one selling to restaurant owners: different buyers, pain, and proof. That difference is the message. [[tool-prospeo]]'s v2 lets you filter on exactly that: who a company sells to, the size of their customers, the department they sell into.

The decision rule: if a data point would change what you say, it is a reason to split into a separate campaign; if it would not, do not split. "Who do they sell to" almost always changes what you say.

## Key takeaways

- Segment by motion (who they sell to), not just firmographics.
- The split test for a campaign: does this attribute change the message? If yes, split; if no, do not.
- Over-investing in copy while under-investing in list precision is why well-written emails still feel generic (commenter Bhuvnesh Upadhyay).
