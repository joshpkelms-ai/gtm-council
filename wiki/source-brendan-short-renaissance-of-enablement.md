# Source: Brendan J Short -- The Renaissance of Enablement (ft. Mercy Bell)

> The Signal newsletter (thesignal.club) by [[expert-brendan-short]], interviewing [[expert-mercy-bell]] (ex-Webflow enablement). Raw: `raw/Clippings/The Renaissance of Enablement.md`. Presented by [[tool-clay]]. Anchors [[framework-three-rs-enablement]].

## Summary

The shaking premise: **we put more care into architecting environments for AI agents than into developing the humans who still run GTM.** The piece unpacks what enablement actually is and what GTM orgs should build for humans in the AI era.

- **Retrieve, recall, resist** -- Mercy's three-R definition of enablement. Most teams over-index on retrieve (systems/content, the easy part RevOps can own) and neglect recall (in-the-moment pattern recognition, what separates top performers, built by repetition not a KB article) and resist (judgment, impulse control, knowing when not to oversell or to walk from a non-ICP deal). See [[framework-three-rs-enablement]].
- **The 2010s playbook is dead.** Content person + systems person + trainer made sense when playbooks were stable. Survivors go one of two ways: into systems/ops (GTM engineering, RevOps) or into coaching, recall-building, and leadership development. The generalist enablement hire is over; diagnose the root cause (PMM gap, ICP clarity, managers who can't coach) and hire a specialist.
- **The agent parallel.** We build agent context windows, give examples of "good", grade outputs one by one. Are we doing the same for humans? Most people are "burnt out, anxious, confused". Execution is the moat; leaders must get honest about where their people are, not just the tech stack. (The agent side of this analogy is [[framework-ai-employee-loop]].)
- **Sales teams will get small.** In 5-7 years, fewer reps; managers become true player-coaches; investment in human skills rises, especially in complex relationship-driven deals. "The art of the one-on-one."

## Self-determination theory for GTM

From Deci and Ryan (1960s onward): three needs -- autonomy (meaningful choice), competence (with a trap: AI gives an addictive *sensation* of competence that isn't actual competence), relatedness (genuine human connection; teams that foster it outperform). A motivation continuum runs from amotivation through external regulation to intrinsic motivation; "companies crushing it are almost exclusively in the identified-to-integrated range. You can't run your business on fear right now."

## Four programs every GTM org needs

1. **Ramp (not onboarding)** -- gradual "can't do anything" to "can hit the number"; build around the customer journey, not the product; no role plays in the first two weeks, instead "train their ear" with 60-70+ clips of real calls (prospect speaking only), stop-and-ask. Especially for high-trust, six/seven-figure cycles.
2. **Product/solution depth** -- by role and altitude; AE, SDR, and SE need different depths.
3. **Skill development** -- discovery, demo, negotiation as always-on muscles with repetition and feedback, not a quarterly drop-in.
4. **Leadership development** -- everything above is delivered by frontline managers; if they aren't developed, none of it works.

## Key takeaways

- The best GTM orgs in 18 months will have the best *human* infrastructure too, applying eval-grade rigour to ramp, coaching, and leadership. Over-indexing on agents while neglecting humans loses.
- A deliberate counterweight to the agent-everything narrative; the inverse of [[concept-borrow-from-engineering]] (don't only borrow agent patterns for machines, apply the same care to people).
