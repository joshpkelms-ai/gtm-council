# Source: Wesley Hoang -- Winning copy strategy and the three cold-email mistakes

> LinkedIn post plus an annotated workflow visual (transcribed) by [[expert-wesley-hoang]]. Raw: `raw/Wesley Hoang/2.md`.

## Summary

Three things B2B founders get wrong about cold email:
1. They test 50 variations at once and learn nothing.
2. They optimise subject lines before validating their core message.
3. They never isolate the offer, the one variable that actually determines if someone responds.

Tweaking subject lines and send times is optimising, not testing. Testing is the big stuff: value prop, pain point, offer. If you are not getting replies, do not rewrite the email -- change what you offer at the end (demo did not work? try a case study, a quick call, a coffee chat). Once something hits, optimise it, then scale.

The visual lays out a sequential testing funnel (the [[framework-cold-email-copy-testing]]):
- Step 1: test 2-3 angles with 2 templates, pick the winning angle.
- Step 2: test 3 copy structures with the winning angle.
- Step 3: test 3 CTAs with the winning angle and structure.
- Step 4: subject line ("not needed in most cases").
- Output: best-performing copy.

## Key takeaways

- Isolate one variable per round; test angle, structure, then CTA in that order. See [[framework-cold-email-copy-testing]].
- The offer is the highest-leverage variable; change it before rewriting copy.
- Subject lines are a late, often-skippable optimisation, not a test.
