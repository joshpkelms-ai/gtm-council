# Concept: Earn the right to automate

> [[expert-brandon-camhi]] and [[expert-noah-adelstein]] (ex-Rippling, now Netic): prove a motion works manually before you scale it with AI. "Go prove the ceiling is sufficiently high before you think about all the automation." The companies that automate well are the ones that did it manually first, learned what worked, then built systems around the proven playbook.

## The sequencing rule

Not anti-automation -- correctly-sequenced automation. The SDRs accumulate context and pattern-match; only once the playbook is proven does the GTM engineer codify it. "Even if we thought we could use AI to automate everything... the priority is learning whether outbound works." If something does not work manually, automating it definitely will not.

## The corollary: automated mediocrity

The failure mode is taking the C+ or B- version of a workflow and scaling it with AI. "People automated mediocrity." Noah's challenge: have you sent a single manual email that got a positive reply? If not, why send 100,000? AI slop has raised the cost of being generic -- the teams that find the A+ version, write it by hand, prove the message, and *then* automate are the ones that cut through. Everyone else produces more noise at higher volume.

## Where humans stay in the loop

- **Context generation is a human job.** A dedicated full-timer accumulates market context far faster than a GTME splitting time across eight projects. Reps generating context in a tight weekly "Shark Tank" loop feed the system that gets codified later.
- **Intuition resists templating.** Two CEOs, one with a digital presence and one without, get handled completely differently -- a judgment you cannot templatise on day one.
