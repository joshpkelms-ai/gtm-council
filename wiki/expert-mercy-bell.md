# Mercy Bell

> Sales enablement and RevOps practitioner with ~15 years across Meta, Outreach, Retool, and Webflow (where she ran Enablement); started as an SDR at Google, then AE at Kahuna; Stanford alum. Her thesis: we architect environments for AI agents with more care than we develop the humans who still run GTM. Originator of the "retrieve, recall, resist" enablement model and a four-program human-development architecture. Interviewed by [[expert-brendan-short]].

## Who she is

Long-time enablement leader and consultant. Reads academic psychology for fun; grounds her frameworks in self-determination theory.

## Core positions

- **Retrieve, recall, resist.** The three jobs of enablement; most teams over-invest in retrieve (systems/content) and neglect recall (in-the-moment pattern recognition) and resist (judgment). See [[framework-three-rs-enablement]].
- **The agent parallel.** The care we put into agent context windows, examples, and graded outputs should also go into ramp, coaching, and leadership development for humans. See [[framework-ai-employee-loop]] for the agent side of the analogy.
- **Sales teams will get small; managers become player-coaches.** In 5-7 years, fewer reps, more investment in human skill, especially in complex relationship-driven deals. "The art of the one-on-one" matters more than the dashboard.
- **Self-determination theory for GTM:** autonomy, competence (beware AI's false sense of competence), relatedness. Run teams from intrinsic motivation, not fear.
- **Four programs every GTM org needs:** ramp (not onboarding), product/solution depth by role, always-on skill development, and leadership development.
