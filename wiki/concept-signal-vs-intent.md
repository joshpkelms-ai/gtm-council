# Concept: Signal is not intent

> [[expert-sachin-jha]]: intent data tells you who *might* be interested; signal tells you who is *already moving*, and *why*. They are not interchangeable, and the difference decides whether outreach lands.

## The distinction

Intent data (whitepaper downloads, broad topic surges) is a weak proxy for a future buyer. Signal is a live event with a short window: someone struggling with a Kubernetes deployment this week, entering a FedRAMP cycle, changing their stack. Approach two months early and you get no response; two months late and they've already chosen. Nobody downloads a whitepaper hoping an SDR will call.

## Signal hoarding

The common failure is not a lack of signals but too many with no workflow: Common Room, six enrichment tools, job-change/funding/competitor alerts, beautiful dashboards, and then no connection between signal and the next conversation. The discipline is to sort signals by tier and act accordingly -- see [[framework-signal-intelligence-layer]].

## Signal changes how you show up

The most actionable point: signal should influence *how* you show up, not just *who* you target. With the signal, the opening line, the assumptions, and the questions all change. Without it, you write generic copy. This is the same logic as [[concept-list-is-the-message]]: if a data point changes the message, it should change the campaign.

## Decaying infrastructure

The signals many playbooks rest on are decaying: roughly half of website visitors are now bots/agents, and search is migrating from Google to AI assistants. Running GTM on visitor tracking and broad intent vendors alone is building on eroding ground. The buyer-side version of this shift is [[concept-ai-first-buyer-journey]].

## The human in the loop

The signal-to-implication leap (the same hiring signal means different things to different companies) is the GTM engineer's judgment and is "not automatable yet". Software to see, humans to act.

## Once you stop hoarding, you must route

Acting on signals at volume needs a logic layer between the signal stack and execution, or multiple triggers collide into duplicate, conflicting outreach. That layer is [[framework-sales-orchestration]] and its six-step routing engine (validate, dedupe, prioritise, route, pace, monitor): the operational complement to this concept.
